The core question

How do rules, social expectations and business support systems shape women's access to and participation in selected high-growth sectors in Nigeria?

Women entrepreneurs play an important role in Nigeria's economy, yet they remain underrepresented in industries associated with innovation, investment and economic growth. Existing research highlights inequalities in women's access to finance, business opportunities and support. Less is known about how women enter and participate in these industries, why their experiences differ, and how government policies, social expectations and business support systems shape their opportunities.

In plain terms: how do women entrepreneurs navigate entering, participating in and growing businesses in these sectors?

The study at a glance

How institutions, ecosystems and women’s pathways connect

What the study investigates

How formal rules, informal expectations and the investors, networks and support organisations around businesses shape women's opportunities to enter, participate and grow. It considers access to finance, markets, regulation, procurement, support organisations, networks and legitimacy, and how these vary by sector and by women's roles as founders, owners and leaders.

It also explores how investors, business networks and support organisations either reinforce barriers or help women overcome them.

What we mean by a high-growth sector

A sector associated with innovation, scaling, investment or structural transformation. This is different from an individual firm that meets a growth-rate threshold. The study focuses on sectors that show a credible orientation towards innovation, growth, investment, market expansion or scale.

How the findings will be used

The findings will be used to produce outputs such as reports, academic papers, book chapters and case studies. They aim to improve understanding of how women entrepreneurs in Nigeria navigate rules, practices, networks and support arrangements to enter, participate in and grow businesses in high-growth sectors.

Ethics approval

This study has been reviewed by the Ethics Office, Henley Business School, University of Reading, UK, and has been given a favourable ethical approval for conduct.

What is the purpose of the study?

The study examines how formal rules, informal expectations and entrepreneurial ecosystem arrangements shape women's opportunity to enter, participate and grow within selected high-growth sectors in Nigeria. It considers access to finance, markets, regulation, procurement, support organizations, networks and legitimacy, and how these conditions may vary by sector and by women's roles as founders, owners and leaders.

Why have you been invited to take part?

You may have been invited because you are aged 18 or over and have direct experience relevant to entrepreneurship in a high-growth sector in Nigeria. You may be a woman who is or has been a founder, co-founder, owner, co-owner or strategic leader; a woman whose attempt to enter or build a venture was stalled, blocked or ended; an ecosystem actor who allocates or facilitates resources or opportunities; or a policy or sector informant with direct design, implementation, regulatory or coordination knowledge.

The study focuses on sectors that show a credible orientation towards innovation, growth, investment, market expansion or scale.

High-growth sector: a sector associated with innovation, scaling, investment or structural transformation; distinct from a firm that meets a growth-rate threshold.

What will participation involve, and how long will it take?

You will be first asked to complete a short pre-screening questionnaire, which should take about 5 to 7 minutes. It helps identify the most relevant interview guide and supports a varied sample. Following this, a semi-structured interview will be conducted in person or online depending on what is most practical.

You will be asked about your role, significant events and decisions, access to resources or opportunities, formal requirements, informal expectations, ecosystem relationships and support arrangements. You may be invited to discuss a simple timeline or process. You are not expected to disclose confidential information that you are not authorised to share, or identify third parties unnecessarily. With your permission, the interview will be audio recorded so that an accurate transcript can be prepared. You may take part without video. Recording will not begin until your consent is confirmed.

What information will be required from me?

The study may collect your name, gender and contact details for recruitment and scheduling as well as support the description of the demographics of our sample; screening information about eligibility, role, sector, location and relevant experience; the audio recording and transcript of the interview; and optional background information used to understand sample variation. You may choose 'prefer not to say' for optional questions.

Contact details, consent records and research data will be kept separately. Research files will use a participant code rather than your name.

How will the data be stored and used?

All data will be anonymised, kept confidential and securely stored, with only anonymous identifiers used where relevant.

All data will be securely stored on the researcher's university OneDrive for security and never shared with anyone outside the research team. Neither your name nor your organisation's name will be included in the research findings. Direct quotes may be used, but they will remain anonymous. The research team anticipates using the research findings to produce outputs like reports, academic papers, book chapters and case studies.

See Privacy and data for who is responsible for your data and your rights.

Are there any risks, costs or benefits?

There are no foreseeable risks or disadvantages expected from taking part in this study.

Your participation is voluntary and there is no cost, reimbursement or compensation involved. You may withdraw at any time without having to give any reason.

While there is no direct personal benefit, your participation will contribute to improving the understanding of how women entrepreneurs in Nigeria navigate rules, practices, networks and support arrangements to enter, participate and grow businesses in selected high-growth sectors.
